Overview

A video world simulator is useful when it predicts what an action does to the scene, not only how the acting body moves. ASTER learns from 13 robot, human and simulated sources through one shared action: point motion on the controlled body — the mover — plus camera geometry. The mover's motion is specified; everything it affects is left for the model to predict.

  • 8Bvideo world simulator
  • 13robot, human and simulated sources
  • 19.3%lower prediction error than the same backbone, on average

Method

One action for every source

Robots, hands, tools and physics all describe what they do the same way: the 3D motion of points on the mover, and the camera. Each source converts to this form with what it already records — forward kinematics for robots, hand and tool tracks for human video, ground-truth trajectories for simulators.

How the action enters

Each mover point takes its appearance from the first frame and carries it along its own path; the camera becomes a Plücker-ray field on the same token grid. Both are added once to the video tokens through a zero-initialised projection, and every block adapts its key and value projections to read them — so training starts from the Cosmos 3 video prior.

Five ways to write an action

Anything that yields point motion and a camera can drive the model: a URDF and its joint trajectory, a retargeted human hand or body, a program written by a coding agent, points dragged in a browser, or tracks lifted out of a video.

The Model Uses the Action
