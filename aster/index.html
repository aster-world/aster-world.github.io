Method

One action for every source

Robots, hands, tools and physics all describe what they do the same way: the 3D motion of points on the mover, and the camera. Each source converts to this form with what it already records: forward kinematics for robots, hand and tool tracks for human video, ground-truth trajectories for simulators.

How the action enters

Each mover point takes its appearance from the first frame and carries it along its own path; the camera becomes a Plücker-ray field on the same token grid. Both are added once to the video tokens through a zero-initialized projection, and every block adapts its key and value projections to read them, so training starts from the Cosmos 3 video prior.

Five ways to write an action

Anything that yields point motion and a camera can drive the model: a URDF and its joint trajectory, a retargeted human hand or body, a program written by a coding agent, points dragged in a browser, or tracks lifted out of a video.

Pre-Training Data

ASTER is pre-trained on one mixture of 13 sources, about 80,000 hours of video, nearly a decade: robot arms and humanoids, human hands at work, and objects and scenes in motion, real and simulated. Every source is converted to the same action before it joins the mixture, the 3D motion of whatever moves plus the camera, so one model learns from all of them.
